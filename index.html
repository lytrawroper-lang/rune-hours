      Wheel of the Year

        The wheel of the year is a modern calendar of eight festivals, put together in the twentieth century from older Gaelic, Norse and English seasonal feasts. The solstices and equinoxes move by a day or so from year to year, so those four dates are approximate. All dates are for the northern half of the world.

          The Three Families

          The 24 runes are traditionally split into three groups of eight, each called an aett, which means a family or a group of eight. The names used for them come from later Icelandic tradition, and each is named after the god linked with its first rune.

          Freyr's aett

          Named for Freyr, the god of harvests, peace and plenty; some name it for his sister Freyja instead. These runes deal with the things a household needs: wealth, strength, protection, speech, travel, light, gifts and joy.

          Heimdall's aett

          Named for Heimdall, the watchman of the gods; it is also called Hagal's aett after its first rune. These runes deal with forces nobody controls: hail, need, ice, the turning year, death and endurance, chance, danger and the sun.

          Tyr's aett

          Named for Tyr, the god of law and battle. These runes deal with people and how they live together: justice, birth, partnership, humankind, water, the seed, the day and the family home.

          Rune History

          Runes are an alphabet first, used by Germanic-speaking peoples for well over a thousand years. Most scholars think they were modelled on a Mediterranean alphabet, either Latin or one of the north Italic scripts.

          • About 150The oldest known runic inscriptions are carved on combs, weapons and jewellery in what is now Denmark and northern Germany. This first alphabet of 24 runes is the Elder Futhark, named after its first six letters.
          • About 400The Kylver stone on the Swedish island of Gotland is carved with the whole rune row in order, the earliest complete futhark that survives.
          • 400s onwardSettlers take runes to England and Frisia, where new runes are added for new sounds. The Anglo-Saxon futhorc grows to around 30 runes.
          • About 750In Scandinavia the row is cut down to 16 runes, the Younger Futhark. This is the alphabet of the Viking Age.
          • About 965King Harald Bluetooth raises the great Jelling stone in Denmark in memory of his parents.
          • 1000sThe height of the runestone fashion. Thousands are raised, most of them in Sweden, usually to remember the dead, and many were painted in bright colours.
          • 1100s to 1300sRunes carry on beside the Latin alphabet for everyday notes. Hundreds of wooden sticks found in Bergen in Norway carry trade tags, prayers, love messages and jokes.
          • 1600sScholars such as Johannes Bureus in Sweden and Ole Worm in Denmark begin collecting and studying the old inscriptions.
          • Up to about 1900In the Swedish region of Dalarna, a local mix of runes and Latin letters stays in use into modern times.
